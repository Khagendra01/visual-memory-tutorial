A box in an image does not directly tell us where an object is in a room. We need camera calibration, depth, and camera pose. First use the inverse camera intrinsics to turn a pixel into a ray. Multiply that ray by optical-axis depth, then rotate and translate the point into the world coordinate system. In the worked example, the camera-space point is point two, point one, and two meters. Adding a one meter translation produces one point two, point one, and two meters. Pure monocular geometry has a scale ambiguity. Inertial measurements or depth can help under the right conditions. SLAM can estimate a sparse landmark map without reconstructing every surface. When reopening an application, align the new session with the saved map before subtracting coordinates. If relocalization fails, use the evidence image and room label instead of a precise arrow.
